HFGCS Monitoring Project

A hobby project that listens to the public voice channels of the U.S. Air Force High Frequency Global Communications System (HFGCS) and studies when and how often its broadcasts are heard.

What is HFGCS?

HFGCS is a worldwide network of high-power shortwave stations. Its voice traffic is sent in the clear on upper sideband, so anyone with a shortwave receiver can hear it. The best-known transmissions are Emergency Action Messages (EAMs): an operator reads a string of phonetic letters and numbers, usually twice. Their content is encrypted, and nobody outside the intended audience knows what an individual message means.

What this project does

  • Listens to the published HFGCS channels (4724, 6739, 8992, 11175, 13200 and 15016 kHz USB) with a software-defined radio.
  • Detects activity with an adaptive noise floor and squelch, so only real voice traffic is kept.
  • Decodes the phonetic alphabet automatically and checks the result against human listening.
  • Counts and compares: daily message counts, rebroadcasts of the same message, and how our log lines up with other hobbyist logs.

We do not try to decrypt anything. The interest is in the signals and the statistics: timing, frequency use, message lengths and repeat patterns.

The live waterfall

The waterfall shows the radio spectrum around the HFGCS channels in real time. Voice transmissions appear as bright vertical bands. It is the quickest way to see whether anything is on the air right now.

What you will not find here

This page is a project overview only. It does not publish audio clips, message logs, or the receiving station's whereabouts. The software is open source, so you can run your own receiver and see the same public signals for yourself.
